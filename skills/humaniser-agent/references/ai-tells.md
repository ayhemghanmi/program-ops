# AI Tells — what to remove and what to do instead

## Words and phrases to cut or replace
- Inflated verbs: delve, leverage, harness, unlock, empower, elevate, foster, streamline, spearhead, navigate (figurative) → use, help, improve, run, lead, handle.
- Filler adjectives: robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, transformative, holistic, dynamic, pivotal, vibrant, innovative (unless literal) → cut, or replace with the specific fact.
- Throat-clearing openers: "I hope this email finds you well", "In today's fast-paced world", "It's worth noting that", "As we embark on" → start with the point.
- Closers: "Feel free to reach out", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Looking forward to hearing from you" (all three together) → one specific next step.
- Hype nouns: landscape, ecosystem (unless literal), journey (unless the program literally uses it), tapestry, realm, synergy.

## Structures to break
- "Not just X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y" → state Y.
- Reflexive rule of three ("fast, flexible, and future-ready") → keep the one or two that matter.
- Every paragraph the same length; every sentence medium-length → mix short and long.
- Bullet lists for content that is really an argument → write a sentence.
- Bold scattered through prose → bold only the one thing the reader must not miss, if any.
- Heavy em-dash use → commas, colons or full stops; at most one em dash per paragraph.
- Colon headers on every line ("Key takeaway: …") → plain sentences.
- Summary sentence repeating the paragraph at the end → delete it.
- Rhetorical questions as transitions ("So what does this mean?") → just say it.

## Footers to remove
Strip boilerplate that sits at the bottom of a document, slide, email or message and adds nothing for the reader:
- Running labels repeated on every page or slide ("Program X · Guide", "Internal use", the document title again).
- Decorative footer bands, taglines and slogans ("Accelerating ideas. Building impact.") unless a brand template requires them.
- AI or tool credits ("Generated with…", "Drafted by AI").
- Stacked sign-offs and disclaimers ("Best regards" + "Kind regards", or a confidentiality notice nobody asked for).
- Trailing "Sources" or "Notes" blocks in messages that don't need them.

Keep only what the reader actually needs: page or slide numbers on long documents, a single sign-off, and legally or contractually required notices (confidentiality on client documents, for example). When unsure whether a footer is required, keep it and mention it in the notes.

## Make it specific
- Replace abstractions with the concrete thing: "significant progress" → "3 of 5 teams signed pilots".
- Name the person, the date, the number.
- Prefer active voice with a real subject: "The committee approved" not "Approval was granted".
- Put the ask or the decision in the first two lines of any email.

## Before / after

Before: "I hope this message finds you well. I'm excited to share that our cohort has made significant strides in their journey, leveraging the robust support of our mentors to unlock new opportunities."
After: "Quick update: four of the six teams closed pilots this month, each with mentor support on the deal. Details below."

Before: "This isn't just a program — it's a transformative ecosystem designed to empower founders."
After: "The program gives each founder a mentor, a 12-week plan and direct introductions to buyers."
